UAE sales careers

Sales jobs in the UAE

By Middle East Job Hub Editorial Team · Last reviewed: November 21, 2026 · Informational guidance
What changed: expanded from a short search overview into a full role-family guide with sales-motion search terms, sector context, CV evidence examples, interview preparation and offer checks.

Sales titles vary widely across sectors. Narrow a UAE search by customer, territory, sales motion and evidence of results rather than relying on a generic sales keyword, and read the pay structure before you commit to an offer.

Read the sector before the title

The same title means different work in different UAE sectors. Real estate sales often runs on commission-heavy structures and evening or weekend client work. Logistics and freight sales reward route and trade-lane knowledge. SaaS and technology sales expect CRM fluency and longer cycles. Hospitality and events sales depend on seasonal calendars and corporate accounts. Financial and insurance products carry their own regulatory expectations. Before applying, decide which sectors your evidence fits and say so in the first lines of your CV rather than presenting yourself as sector-neutral.

Define the territory and customer

A role can be based in Dubai or Abu Dhabi while serving customers across the UAE or wider Gulf. Read whether the job is inbound, outbound, field based, partner led or enterprise focused. Record travel expectations, client-site requirements, language requirements and the named market before applying. Arabic, Hindi, Urdu, Tagalog, Russian or Mandarin can each be a genuine requirement depending on the customer base, so treat language lines in a vacancy as real criteria, not decoration.

Show credible commercial evidence

Sales CVs fail in two directions: no numbers, or numbers nobody believes. Compare these lines:

  • Weak: "Exceeded sales targets and managed key clients."
  • Stronger: "Carried an annual quota for a logistics territory covering Dubai and Sharjah; closed 18 new accounts in 2025 and renewed 90% of an existing book of 40 clients."

The stronger line gives the period, segment, scope and result without an inflated percentage, and it invites questions you can actually answer. If your work was relationship or operations heavy rather than quota carrying, evidence it the same way: accounts supported, order values, complaint resolution, repeat business. Avoid unsupported revenue claims; experienced interviewers test them within minutes. The UAE CV guide has a fuller evidence checklist.

Understand the pay structure before you accept

UAE sales offers commonly combine a base salary with commission, allowances or both. Before accepting, ask for the commission plan in writing: what it pays on, when it is paid, what happens to pipeline if you leave, and whether targets are individual or team based. A low base with an unwritten commission promise is a risk you should price honestly. Check the written offer against the vacancy you applied for, and use official routes such as the MOHRE offer inquiry where applicable. Under UAE rules, recruitment costs are generally the employer's responsibility; a request for money to secure a sales job is a warning sign, whatever the promised earnings.

Prepare for the interview

Expect a role-play or a "sell me this" exercise in many UAE sales interviews, plus questions about your pipeline. Prepare a two-minute account of your best sales period with numbers you can defend, a lost deal and what it taught you, and a clear view of how you would approach this employer's market in your first 90 days. For field roles, be ready to discuss how you plan a territory week.
