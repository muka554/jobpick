Which role should you explore?

How the shortlist works

This is an original JobPick navigation exercise, not a validated psychometric assessment or professional career advice. Every answer other than Not sure maps to one role family and adds one count. There are no hidden weights, demographic questions, personality labels, labour-market forecasts or salary inputs. The wording describes broad examples of work, not every job in that family.

Results show only families you selected, highest count first. Equal counts are ties, shown in a fixed editorial order with no implied preference. Four different answers produce four tied suggestions. Four Not sure answers produce no ranking. A family with no selections is not unsuitable for you; it simply did not receive a count in this small set of questions.

A repeated preference may be worth exploring, but it does not prove ability or fit. If one answer matters much more than the others, use your own judgement rather than treating the tally as a decision. No employer receives your answers, and the tool does not connect you to a vacancy.

Turn an interest into a useful next step

  1. Read one suggested guide and one alternative. Write down the actual work that appeals to you, not only the job title.
  2. Find a current vacancy through a verified employer or a suitable job source. Compare duties, qualifications, language, location, schedule and experience requirements.
  3. Identify one real example or learning project that shows relevant work. Do not add a skill to your CV because a quiz suggested a role.
  4. Check constraints separately: qualifications, licensing, work authorisation, accessibility needs, shifts, commute and package. This tool asks none of those questions and cannot decide whether a role is available or accessible to you.

Healthcare interests do not establish eligibility for clinical practice. Technical interests do not demonstrate technical competence. For any regulated or specialist work, check the actual role and the appropriate authority independently.

Method and review record

Reviewed 21 November 2026. First release: four editorial interest questions, eight guide destinations, an equal-count tally and a no-signal result. The exercise is designed to make our existing role library easier to browse. It is not based on a licensed interest inventory, hiring dataset or personality model. Role descriptions are broad editorial summaries linked to their full guides, where source details and practical limits are given.

No claims are made about scientific validity, expected earnings or hiring outcomes. If a guide link or description needs a correction, use our corrections page, without sending identity documents, CVs or confidential records.
