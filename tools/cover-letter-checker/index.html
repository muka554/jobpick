Review an existing letter

For English, non-academic job letters. Follow the employer's instructions first. Paste plain text, without confidential records or identity-document details. Nothing is submitted to an employer.

At least 30 words, at most 12,000 characters. The tool does not check spelling, grammar, truth or final page layout.

For literal text comparison only. Different valid wording or spelling may not match.

Up to six short requirements, one per line; 1,200 characters total. This checks whether text appears, not whether you have a skill. Do not add claims just to make a phrase match.

What the checker can and cannot tell you

This is a set of simple, visible text rules, not an AI assessment. It counts words and blank-line text blocks, checks whether the optional role and employer text appears, looks for a small list of placeholders and stock phrases, and flags possible sentences over 40 words. A small English verb list provides a reminder to inspect your examples. Finding a verb does not prove that an example is good or true; not finding one does not mean your letter lacks evidence.

The 180–450-word range and long-sentence threshold are JobPick editorial prompts, not employer rules or university-prescribed cut-offs. A short application message may be appropriate; an academic letter may need a different format entirely. The checker cannot assess suitability, eligibility, hiring chances, ATS acceptance, tone in context, grammar, factual accuracy, confidentiality or whether the final document fits one page.

Use the review without inventing experience

  1. Read the original vacancy and select a few requirements that actually matter to the role.
  2. Explain why this particular work and employer interest you, based on information you have checked.
  3. Choose one or two real examples. State the situation, your own action and a result or useful scope you can support. A precise description is better than a made-up percentage.
  4. Use the review as a set of questions. A phrase match is not a reason to add a skill, employer, qualification or achievement you do not have.
  5. Check names, dates, contact details and instructions yourself. Read the letter aloud, then open the actual attachment or portal preview before sending.

A truthful before-and-after pattern

Vague: "I am a hardworking team player with excellent communication skills."

Illustrative wording, use only if true: "I handled booking-change enquiries, checked the reservation record and referred rate exceptions to the authorised supervisor." This names a task and a boundary without inventing revenue or satisfaction figures. It is a fictional teaching example, not a candidate story or a sentence the checker generates for you.
