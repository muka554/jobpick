IT jobs in the UAE

By Middle East Job Hub Editorial Team · Last reviewed: November 21, 2026 · Informational guidance
What changed: expanded from a short search overview into a full role-family guide with discipline-specific search terms, employer types, CV evidence examples, interview preparation and verification steps.

A strong IT search connects the role title to a stack, delivery context and city. This guide helps you build a specific search for technology work in Dubai, Abu Dhabi and across the UAE, prepare evidence an employer can test, and verify the original vacancy before you share personal documents.

Know who hires for IT in the UAE

Technology roles in the UAE come from several distinct employer types, and each hires differently:

  • Banks and fintech. Large technology teams with structured recruitment, formal job grades and longer interview processes. Expect security screening and detailed verification of employment history.
  • Telecoms and technology groups. Operators and regional tech groups hire for network, cloud, data and product roles, often through their own careers portals first.
  • Government and semi-government digital teams. Dubai and federal entities run official portals such as Dubai Careers. Public routes may have separate eligibility and account requirements; read the role page on the portal itself.
  • Aviation, logistics and retail groups. The UAE's large aviation, ports and retail groups run substantial in-house technology teams and list roles on their own career sites.
  • Consultancies and managed service providers. These hire for client-site work. Check whether the role is on the consultancy's payroll and which client site you would serve before applying.
  • Startups and free-zone companies. Dubai's free zones host clusters of technology companies. Smaller employers may hire faster but verify the entity carefully, especially if you found the role through a message rather than a careers page.

Use Dubai and Abu Dhabi context

For Dubai jobs, compare private-sector employer pages with Dubai Government routes where relevant. For Abu Dhabi jobs, check the named worksite and whether the role is office, client, industrial or site based. City and worksite are separate fields in your application log. A "Dubai" title can mean a client site in another emirate, a data centre outside the city, or a hybrid arrangement that only becomes clear in the interview.

Show evidence, not a keyword list

A technology CV is more credible when it explains what changed. Compare these two lines:

  • Weak: "Responsible for cloud infrastructure and DevOps."
  • Stronger: "Migrated 40 internal services to a container platform on a named cloud provider; cut deployment time from hours to minutes and owned the incident runbook used by a 6-person team."

The stronger line names the system, your responsibility, the scale and the result, and it gives an interviewer something to test. Keep certifications and years of experience accurate, and link portfolio work only when it is safe to share publicly. If a project was under NDA, describe the shape of the problem and your role without naming the client. The UAE CV guide has a fuller evidence checklist.

Prepare for the interview format

UAE technology interviews commonly combine a screening call, a technical assessment and a panel round. Prepare three things in advance: a two-minute account of your most relevant project with numbers you can defend; a short explanation of a failure and what you changed afterwards; and questions that show you read the vacancy, such as the team structure, the stack in production and how success is measured in the first months. For consultancy or outsourcing roles, also ask which entity employs you and where the client site is.

Check offers and paperwork carefully

Under UAE rules, recruitment costs are generally the employer's responsibility, and a request for money in exchange for a job, visa or interview is a serious warning sign. For a private-sector offer, the official MOHRE offer inquiry route lets you check an offer letter where applicable. Rules change, so confirm current requirements on the UAE Government jobs portal rather than relying on a recruiter's message. Read our employer verification guide before signing anything.

Verify the original application route

Use the live employer listings hub to discover current public-feed roles, but open the original employer page before applying. Check the domain, job reference, location, closing date and contact method. Review the recruitment scam warning signs guide before sharing sensitive documents, and never send passport copies or banking details to an unverified contact at the application stage.
