Application safety, with worked examples

How to check a UAE employer, recruiter or job offer

Separate the company, the contact, the vacancy and the offer. Use an evidence record and three worked cases to decide what to check next, without turning a missing result into a fraud verdict.

By Middle East Job Hub Editorial Team · Published 21 November 2026 · Substantially reviewed 22 November 2026 · Informational guidance

This guide does not certify an employer. A real company can be impersonated, a genuine advert can be copied and a familiar email address can be misused. These checks reduce unanswered questions; they do not guarantee authenticity, employment or work permission. Do not send JobPick a passport, offer letter, bank record or private message for a verdict.

1. Decide what you are trying to establish

Do not put every question into one box called "verified". Record four separate claims: the legal company exists; this person is authorised to contact you; this vacancy belongs to that employer; this offer follows the relevant employment process. Evidence for one claim does not settle the others.

Evidence you can describe

An official record matches a legal name. A careers page carries a particular reference. A contact reached independently confirms the person's role. Save the source, date and exact point supported.

Questions still open

A licence copy was only supplied by the sender. A role has disappeared. The worksite differs. Record the gap and the authority or verified employer contact needed to answer it.

Use three practical outcomes: continue through the independently found route, pause for a specific answer, or stop sharing and seek help. These are your next actions, not authenticity ratings. There is no reliable ten-minute guarantee or checklist score.

2. Find the legal entity and the right authority

Ask for the employing organisation's full legal name, claimed location and licensing authority. Keep the agency name separate from the employer name. A brand, hotel, office building or recruitment consultant may not be the legal entity that would employ you.

The UAE Government's National Economic Register page describes economic-licence, activity and trade-name enquiries and lists licensing authorities. Start there or at the authority said to have issued the licence. Compare the name and available licence details with the proposed employer. A screenshot supplied by the contact is a lead to check, not independent evidence.

If you cannot find a result, check spelling, Arabic/English names, the legal suffix and the issuing authority. Ask that authority what its search covers. Do not conclude that the company is fictitious from a missing result, or that the person contacting you is authorised from a matching company record. We have not tested every register, account requirement or licence record.

For a free-zone employer, identify the relevant free-zone authority before choosing an employment or permit inquiry. The government's recruiting-in-free-zones page describes separate procedures and authority roles, including distinct frameworks for DIFC and ADGM. This guide does not decide which law or service applies to your case. Government employment and domestic work also need their appropriate routes, not an assumed private-sector process.

3. Find the employer's route independently

Find the employer's official website without using only the message's links. Open its careers page and follow any recruitment-system link from there. An application system on another domain is not automatically suspicious; the important connection is whether the employer's own site directs you to it.

  • Compare the actual email domain and destination URL, not the display name, logo or button label.
  • Use a published contact you found independently to ask about an unexplained recruiter, changed destination or requested document.
  • A matching domain is useful context, not proof that a message or attachment is safe. Do not open an unexpected file just to complete this checklist.
  • Keep the vacancy reference and the date checked. A home page alone does not establish a current vacancy.

For an employer-specific example, DHL's recruitment-fraud notice recommends independent verification through DHL and its official careers portal. It also says DHL does not ask candidates to transfer money in advance or purchase services as part of recruitment. That is DHL's published guidance, not a JobPick investigation of any advert or a rule about every employer's contact channels.

4. Check an agency separately

The UAE Government's job-search guidance advises using licensed recruitment agencies and referring to MOHRE to check licensed companies or raise a complaint. Ask for the agency's full legal name, the licence details and the specific role brief. Check through the responsible official route rather than accepting a certificate image as the final answer.

Separately establish that the individual is connected to the agency. Use its independently found contact route and ask whether the person and vacancy reference belong to it. A licence, a professional-network profile and a company email answer different questions. None proves the agency has authority to recruit for the particular employer.

If the client is confidential, record that limitation. Ask what can be disclosed now, whether your CV will be sent to a client and when your consent or further information is needed. Do not pretend to have verified a client that has not been identified. A confidential search is not, by itself, a fraud finding; it is a reason to control what you share and clarify the next step.

5. Compare the actual vacancy

Keep the original title, reference, duties, employer, location, date and application destination together. Compare these with the independently found employer route. A repeated sentence, different salary or missing careers-page entry needs explanation, not an instant verdict: similar adverts may be templates, reposts or agency versions.

Ask a precise question, such as whether reference A is still open and whether the named agency is handling it. Do not replace that with "Is this company real?" If the role is no longer available, do not keep applying through an unexplained message link. Save the discrepancy and use the verified contact route for any clarification.

Check worksite and working pattern separately. An interview address or registered office does not establish your normal workplace, shift, travel requirement or remote-work arrangement. Record what the actual role states and what is still missing.

6. Choose the appropriate offer inquiry

The government's labour-and-visa-fraud guidance points readers to MOHRE enquiries for offer checks. For a MOHRE-handled offer, start at the official Inquiry Service and switch to English if needed, then choose the service labelled Enquiry for Job Offer. For a free-zone or other employment route, first confirm the responsible authority rather than treating one missing MOHRE result as decisive.

Public-form observation, 22 November 2026: the English job-offer inquiry we opened displayed Transaction No, Company No, From Date, To Date, a permit-type selector and a captcha. No details were entered and no result was requested. Other MOHRE offer/contract screens have different labels and fields. Follow the current official service, and ask MOHRE if you are unsure which one applies. JobPick does not collect those details.

Ask the independently verified employer for the reference information needed by the applicable service. If a service returns information, compare the named employer, role and terms with what you were offered; investigate a mismatch through that authority. A returned document is not permission to work, a promise of payment or proof that every person in the conversation is authorised.

Keep the offer version you received and compare it with subsequent contract terms. Ask about changed pay, allowances, worksite, role or conditions before accepting. For current permit or entry requirements, use the relevant official authority. This article is not legal or immigration advice.

7. Pause a payment request

The UAE Government's job-search guidance says job seekers are not required to pay recruitment agencies, and distinguishes optional portal extras from recruitment commission. Its labour-and-visa-fraud guidance states that employers bear recruitment expenses. Do not treat a fee for securing a job, visa processing or a medical test as safe because it is described as refundable.

Stop the payment and further sensitive disclosure while you verify the request independently. Do not pay a small amount as a test, send a code, share banking credentials or buy a service to unlock an interview. An optional CV product offered on a platform is not the same as a recruitment fee; its existence does not validate a job offer or an unrelated payment demand.

Worked case 1 · Fictional exercise, not a live vacancy

A direct-employer advert with a worksite mismatch

Starting information: a board lists a coordinator role for Employer A. Its reference matches a role reached through Employer A's own careers page, but the board says Dubai while the employer page names a different site.

  1. Record the supported point: the independently found employer page carries that reference. Save both URLs and dates. Do not expand this into "the board contact is verified".
  2. Separate the unresolved point: normal worksite and travel are unclear. The registered office does not answer that question.
  3. Choose a next action: follow the employer page's application instructions if the role fits. Ask about the worksite through the employer's independently located contact route before making travel or relocation commitments.

Example record: Reference matched on employer route; location differs; ask employer about normal site and travel. No passport sent to the board contact. This is an illustration of reasoning, not a report that JobPick checked Employer A.

Worked case 2 · Fictional exercise, not an agency endorsement

An agency certificate with an unnamed client

Starting information: Agency B sends a certificate image and requests your CV for a confidential client. The consultant uses a plausible agency address, but you have not established their connection to the agency or its authority for this search.

  1. Check the agency claim: use its legal name and licence details with the responsible official route. A supplied image does not establish current status by itself.
  2. Check the contact claim: contact the agency through details you found independently. Ask whether the consultant and role brief are theirs. Do not rely only on a reply within the original conversation.
  3. Keep the client gap visible: ask what can be disclosed, who would receive your CV and what the next stage involves. Do not mark the unnamed employer as checked.

Example record: Agency status and consultant connection need independent confirmation; client undisclosed; pause identity-document sharing and clarify the intended CV recipient. If you later share a CV, use an application version without unnecessary identity information. No actual licence or individual was investigated for this exercise.

Worked case 3 · Fictional exercise, not a finding about a real sender

A copied brand and an urgent processing-fee demand

Starting information: a message carries a recognisable employer logo and promises an offer after a refundable processing payment to a personal account. The deadline is today. You have not independently connected the sender to the employer.

  1. Do not test the demand by paying: save the message, sender identifier, payment instructions and date without opening extra attachments or following its verification links.
  2. Separate the evidence: a logo and even a real company record do not connect this sender to that company. The payment demand conflicts with the official recruitment-expense guidance above.
  3. Choose a safer route: stop sharing and contact the real employer through independently found details. Use official reporting/support channels if needed. Do not argue with a suspected impersonator or send more documents to prove your identity.

Example record: Sender unverified; payment requested for securing employment; do not pay; preserve evidence and seek independent help. JobPick has not labelled a named business fraudulent or promised a refund.

8. If documents or money were already shared

Preserve the original messages, vacancy URLs, dates, payment instructions and any transaction records privately. Do not publish other people's personal details in a review or send them to JobPick. If money or banking information was involved, contact your bank or payment provider promptly through its official channel. Do not pay a second person who promises recovery.

For recruitment and employment questions, use the support route reached from MOHRE's official website or the relevant authority. For suspected crime, use the applicable police reporting route. The government's fraud-guidance page is an official starting point for employment and visa enquiries. We do not promise that any channel will accept your case, recover money or reach a particular outcome.

Report the advert to the platform where you found it as a separate step. A platform report is not a substitute for contacting the appropriate authority or payment provider.

9. Make your next-action record

For each approach, keep a private note with these fields. Do not store identity-document numbers in a general search spreadsheet.

  • Claim being checked: company, contact, vacancy or offer.
  • Independent source and date: where you went, not only what the sender supplied.
  • What the evidence supports: a specific matching name, reference or confirmation.
  • What remains unknown: authority, contact relationship, terms, worksite or document request.
  • Next action and route: continue through the employer site, pause for a named answer, or stop sharing and seek help.
  • Materials already sent: which CV version or documents, to whom and when.

You have finished a useful check when you can explain the evidence and the next action, not when every box is ticked. Recheck material changes rather than copying an old "verified" label onto a new approach.
