How to Verify a UAE Employer or Recruiter Before You Apply

A practical evidence framework for deciding whether a company, recruitment agency, vacancy or offer is worth your documents and your time, using checks you can run yourself plus the official UAE routes.

By Middle East Job Hub Editorial Team · Published 21 November 2026 · Last reviewed 21 November 2026 · Informational guidance

Read this first: Verification reduces risk; it does not guarantee that a vacancy is genuine or that you will be hired. This guide cannot declare a specific company fraudulent. If you have lost money or shared documents with a suspicious contact, use the official reporting routes in the final section rather than relying on an article.

The case for checking

Why verification is worth ten minutes

Recruitment fraud in the Gulf follows repeatable patterns: copied vacancies from real companies, look-alike domains, messaging-app "interviews", and fees dressed up as processing, training, visa or insurance costs. The checks below take about ten minutes per employer and catch most of these patterns before you share a passport copy or pay anything. They also help with a quieter problem: legitimate but outdated or duplicated listings that waste your applications.

Run the checks in order and stop when you have enough confidence either way. You do not need to prove a company is perfect; you need enough evidence to decide whether this specific vacancy, from this specific contact, deserves your documents.

Step one

Check the company's legal identity

Every company operating in the UAE holds a trade licence issued by an emirate's economic department or a free-zone authority. You do not need the licence number to start; begin with the name.

  1. Fix the exact legal name. From the vacancy, offer or email signature, write down the company's full name as given. Many scams borrow the brand of a real company while trading under a different name, or add words like "international" or "group" to a famous name.
  2. Look the name up in an official register. The UAE maintains public licence lookup services through the economic departments of each emirate and the free-zone authorities. Search the exact name and check that the entity exists, its licence status, and that its activity roughly matches the work described.
  3. Compare what you find. The registered name, location and activity should be consistent with the vacancy. A "hospitality group" that does not appear in any register, or an entity licensed for an unrelated activity, deserves more questions before you proceed.

Start from the UAE Government jobs portal, which links to official employment information, and use the economic-department or free-zone site of the emirate where the company claims to be based. Avoid third-party "company check" sites that ask you to pay or register for basic licence facts.

Step two

Check the website, domain and careers page

A real company's domain is one of its most stable assets, and one of the hardest things for a scammer to fake well.

  • Find the domain independently. Search for the company name yourself and note the official domain. Do not start from a link sent to you in a message.
  • Compare the contact you received. Email from a free mailbox (Gmail, Outlook, Yahoo) claiming to represent a large employer is a warning sign. So is a domain one letter off from the real one, or a look-alike with extra words such as "-careers" or "-hiring" on a recently registered address.
  • Find the vacancy on the careers page. Most medium and large UAE employers list open roles on their own site. If the role you saw on a board or in a message does not appear anywhere on the employer's own careers page, apply through the careers page instead or ask the employer directly.
  • Check the contact route. The careers page's contact details should match the person contacting you. When in doubt, call the company's published switchboard and ask whether the vacancy and the recruiter are real.

Step three

Check a recruiter or agency

Recruitment and employment agencies operating in the UAE are licensed and regulated by the Ministry of Human Resources and Emiratisation (MOHRE). That gives you a concrete check.

  1. Ask for the agency's full name and licence. A legitimate agency will identify itself plainly. You can check MOHRE's services for licensed agencies through the MOHRE services pages or its app.
  2. Check the individual. A recruiter with a verifiable history will usually have a consistent professional profile, a company email address, and a willingness to name the employer or explain why they cannot yet. Be cautious of profiles created recently, with few connections, using a free email address.
  3. Test the process. Real agencies interview, shortlist and arrange employer interviews. A process that moves from first message to "offer" in a day, without ever speaking to the employer, is a pattern to pause on.

Step four

Check the vacancy itself

  • Search the exact wording. Copy a distinctive sentence from the vacancy into a search engine. If it appears under different company names or in different countries, it is a template or a copied listing.
  • Compare dates and duplicates. The same role reposted for many months, or posted simultaneously under several agencies with different pay, deserves caution.
  • Judge the pay against the role. An unusually high salary for undemanding work is a classic lure. If the pay seems too good for the requirements, verify harder before applying.
  • Check what is missing. Genuine vacancies usually state duties, requirements and a location. A vacancy that is all benefits and no substance, or that hides the employer entirely at application stage, gives you nothing to verify.

Step five

Check the offer letter

For private-sector roles in the UAE, MOHRE provides an official route to check an offer letter. Use the MOHRE offer inquiry service where applicable rather than relying on a forwarded PDF or screenshot. Read the written offer against the vacancy you verified: employer name, role, work location, remuneration and conditions should match what you were told. Rules and services change, so confirm the current process on official pages before relying on this guide.

Be careful with "visa first, job later" arrangements described over messaging apps. Employment and entry rules are set by official authorities, and the UAE Government portal is the reliable starting point for current requirements.

The clearest signal

Money requests: the clearest warning sign

Under UAE rules, recruitment costs are generally the employer's responsibility, and licensed agencies are not permitted to charge job seekers for placing them in work. In practice, that means any of the following requests should end the conversation until independently verified:

  • A "processing", "registration", "training" or "medical" fee to secure a job or interview.
  • A request for gift cards, cryptocurrency or a transfer to a personal account.
  • A demand for card details, online-banking passwords or one-time codes.
  • An "advance" on salary you must repay, or payment for equipment before you start.

Fee requests are often paired with urgency: a deadline, a limited quota, a promise that the fee is refundable. Urgency is part of the script. Legitimate employers can wait a day while you check.

When checks fail

If something is wrong: official routes

  • MOHRE handles labour and recruitment complaints, including unlicensed agency activity. Its current contact channels, including the call centre, are listed on the MOHRE services pages.
  • Police e-crime portals. Each emirate's police force operates an online channel for reporting online fraud, including fake job offers. Report with the messages, account details and payment records you kept.
  • The platform. Report the listing to the job board or social platform where you found it so it can be removed for others.

Keep the evidence: the vacancy URL, messages, email headers where you can get them, account numbers you were asked to pay, and any documents you sent. If you shared identity documents with a suspicious contact, act quickly through official channels; do not wait to see what happens.

Run it in ten minutes

The ten-minute checklist

  • I found the company's exact legal name and confirmed it exists in an official UAE register.
  • I found the official domain myself and the vacancy or the employer appears there.
  • The person contacting me uses the company domain or a verifiable licensed agency.
  • The vacancy wording is not copied across unrelated companies, and the pay fits the role.
  • For an agency, the agency is identifiable and MOHRE-licensed.
  • For an offer, I checked it through the MOHRE offer inquiry where applicable.
  • Nobody has asked me for money, gift cards, codes or banking passwords.
  • I saved the vacancy URL, messages and dates in my application log.
