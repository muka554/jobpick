Recruitment Scam Warning Signs

By Ahmed Abayzeed · Middle East Job Hub · Last updated: August 25, 2026

Editorially reviewed for clarity, source context, and internal links on August 25, 2026.

The single most important rule: a legitimate employer never asks you to pay for a job — not for "registration," "training materials," "visa processing," or anything else. Any request for money upfront is a scam, full stop.

The Gulf job market's popularity with international candidates also makes it a target for recruitment fraud, much of it now happening over WhatsApp and Telegram rather than email. Here's what to watch for.

Red flags in how you were contacted

  • An unsolicited message with a job offer you never applied for, especially via WhatsApp or Telegram from an unknown number
  • Vague job details — no clear company name, no specific role responsibilities, salary that seems unusually high for stated experience
  • A "recruiter" using a personal Gmail/Yahoo address instead of a company domain
  • Pressure to respond or decide within hours, or heavy secrecy about the process

Red flags during the "process"

  • Any request for payment — registration fees, "training" costs, visa or medical fees paid by you directly to an individual
  • Requests for your passport copy, bank account details, or other sensitive documents before you've received a formal, signed offer letter
  • No proper interview, or an interview that skips basic questions about your actual experience
  • Being asked to purchase gift cards, cryptocurrency, or wire money to a personal account for any reason connected to "your job"

How to verify a company or offer is real

  1. Search the company name plus "reviews" or "scam" — pattern reports from other candidates are the fastest signal.
  2. Check whether the company has a real, established website and a LinkedIn page with actual employees, not just a logo.
  3. Ask for a formal offer letter on company letterhead, and verify the domain in any email address matches the company's real website.
  4. Request a proper video call interview — scammers are far less willing to do this convincingly.
  5. For UAE roles, legitimate employers process work visas through official government channels, not informal side payments.

If you think you've been targeted

Stop all communication and don't send anything further. Do not click links from unknown recruiters or download attachments from numbers you don't recognize. If you've already shared sensitive documents or sent money, report it to your local police and, in the UAE, through the official eCrime reporting channels. If it happened through a job platform listed on this site, report the profile or listing directly to that platform — most have a reporting mechanism for exactly this.

Application evidence checklist

Treat every application as a traceable process: retain the original vacancy URL, verify the employer through an independent contact route, and pause if anyone asks for money, account credentials, or identity documents before a verified hiring stage.

  • Save the original job URL, employer name, date seen, and application channel in a simple log.
  • Use a role-specific CV and keep copies of the version you submit; do not add qualifications, salary history, or availability claims you cannot support.
  • Confirm interview invitations through the employer’s official careers page or independently found contact details, not only through a message or forwarded link.
  • Do not pay a recruitment fee or share identity, banking, or account credentials until the employer and hiring stage are independently verified.
