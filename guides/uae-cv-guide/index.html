1. Start with the vacancy, not a list of keywords

For a real application, save the employer, title, reference, worksite, deadline and application route first. Separate essential conditions from preferred experience. Oxford's careers guidance recommends matching criteria to specific examples from work, study or other activities. The evidence labels below are JobPick's working method, not a recruiter scoring system.

Fictional vacancy brief

Operations Administrator, Coastline Office Services (fictional)

Practice reference: JP-PRACTICE-01. Worksite: Dubai, on site. This is a teaching brief with no application URL, salary, deadline or open position.

  • Essential: maintain an accurate order-status spreadsheet; write clear customer updates; hand unresolved delivery exceptions to the operations lead; prepare a weekly outstanding-items summary.
  • Preferred: experience with an enterprise resource planning (ERP) system. The brief does not name a product.
  • Application instructions for this exercise: a CV and a supporting statement of no more than 120 words. The email example below is an alternative only if a real employer asks for email applications, not an extra document to send automatically.
  • Unresolved: shift pattern, start date, work-permission requirements and whether training covers the preferred system. Mira must not guess these answers.

Candidate facts for the exercise: Mira Nasser is a fictional former office assistant in Sharjah. She worked at fictional Cedar Parcel Services from September 2024 to October 2026, after completing a fictional Diploma in Business Administration at fictional Bay Learning Institute in June 2024. She used Excel filters and sorting, an order-status log and email. She has not used an ERP system. Her commute, start date and work-permission position have not been established.

Requirement-to-evidence sheet, filled in

These are invented practice notes, not private documents. Keep real source records private; do not send confidential logs to prove a bullet.

Accurate order-status spreadsheet

Evidence: in one practice week, Mira's log lists 12 delivery exceptions. She checked order references and marked missing status information for the lead.

Label: direct. Use: first experience bullet. Twelve is the size of that one log, not a daily average, a success rate or a volume of resolved cases.

Clear customer updates

Evidence: a practice draft explains a delayed order, the confirmed status and the next update. It makes no unsupported arrival promise.

Label: direct. Use: second experience bullet and supporting statement. No satisfaction result was recorded.

Escalation and weekly summary

Evidence: a practice handover names the order reference, last action, owner and next check. Her weekly summary separates open items from those with a confirmed update.

Label: direct. Use: third and fourth bullets. There is no evidence that she managed the operations team or approved refunds.

ERP experience

Evidence: none. Excel work is relevant record-keeping experience, not use of an ERP product.

Label: not yet demonstrated. Use: do not add it to skills. Ask about training if appropriate. If it is essential in a real vacancy, resolve the gap before presenting yourself as qualified.

2. Build a master record, then tailor a copy

Keep accurate employers, titles, dates, qualifications, tools and projects in a private master record. Change the order and emphasis for the vacancy, not the facts. The National Careers Service CV guide supports clear headings, relevant content and recent-first work history. It is a UK writing resource, not a UAE hiring or immigration rule.

Here is the entire fictional CV, not selected bullets with the rest left to imagine. Its experience-first order matches this practice role. A first-job applicant could instead lead with relevant study, projects or volunteering, labelled as such. The sample omits a phone number to avoid publishing a real person's number; a real applicant should include their own working contact details.

3. Improve the wording without improving the facts

Use action + scope + method + result, where known. A result can be a useful handover or a checked record. It need not be a percentage. Oxford's CV guidance supports relevant, evidence-based descriptions. Our choice to leave unmeasured results unquantified is part of this editorial exercise.

Before, after and the evidence boundary

Before: Responsible for customer service and reports.

After: Handed unresolved cases to the operations lead with the order reference, last action, owner and next check.

Why: the practice handover supports this exact task and method. It does not support "led operations", "resolved every complaint" or "improved customer satisfaction by 30%".

  • Kept: the real role title within the fictional story, the same employment dates and the limited Excel skills shown in the notes.
  • Moved forward: order records and handovers, because they match the practice brief.
  • Left out: ERP proficiency, a promotion, team leadership, immediate availability and improved delivery speed. The notes contain none of those facts.
  • Not attached: customer logs or internal emails. A safe interview explanation is different from distributing someone else's records.

If your evidence is a course, a home project or volunteering, say so. A classroom exercise is not production employment. You can describe inputs, steps and output without turning practice into paid work. See the finance, logistics, healthcare or hospitality guide for other evidence settings.

4. Check the actual file a reader receives

Follow the vacancy's required format and size first. Use real text, descriptive headings and a straightforward reading order. Greenhouse's parser guidance identifies image resumes, columns, complex tables and contact details in headers, footers or text boxes as possible parsing problems. That is a vendor's description of its own product, not proof that every system rejects those designs.

Greenhouse also distinguishes supported uploads from successful parsing: its upload guidance lists a 100 MB candidate-file limit, while its parser guidance says it cannot parse resumes larger than 2.5 MB. Do not treat an accepted upload as proof that the fields are correct, or apply either number to every employer.

  1. Open the exported file. Check cut-off text, page breaks, dates, contacts and links.
  2. Select its text and paste into a plain-text editor. Check that headings and experience remain in a useful order. This is a basic extraction check, not an ATS certification.
  3. Check the actual portal's extracted fields if it shows them. Correct errors before submission where allowed.
  4. Read it on a phone. A decorative sidebar should not hide the only copy of your contact details.
  5. Save the exact version you plan to send, with a clear filename such as Mira-Nasser-Operations-Administrator.pdf for this exercise.

The sample uses one column for clarity. We have not submitted it to Greenhouse or another hiring system. Fictional data itself can affect parsing, as Greenhouse's guidance notes. There is no universal ATS pass score here, no invisible keywords and no claim that this PDF passes an employer's screening.

5. Add personal context carefully

State city, notice period, availability and relocation plans accurately when relevant or requested. Mira's on-site commute, start date and permission to work are unresolved in this exercise, so they are not asserted in her CV. An application is not evidence of work authorisation. Verify your own situation through the relevant official service or qualified adviser.

Do not assume that every UAE CV needs a photo, nationality, birth date, passport number or identity-document scan. Share only what the legitimate application needs at that stage. Check the recipient and purpose before sending extra documents. Keep passport scans and other people's contact details out of a widely circulated CV. A clinical evaluation is not the same as a licence; see the healthcare guide for that distinction.

6. Use tools as prompts, not verdicts

Use the tools directory to compare the available CV and application tools before choosing one. Read each tool's limits and privacy information; the basic checklist and the separate signed-in workspace do not have the same processing model.

The basic CV checklist looks for a small set of text signals. It cannot verify your history, assess your professional competence or predict a recruiter's decision. Its coverage percentage is not an ATS score. A detected heading does not prove the content is good.

If you choose the separate AI-assisted workspace, read the privacy information and upload screen before sharing a document. Do not upload confidential employer information. Compare every generated sentence with your source record, especially dates, qualifications, employers and numbers. Scores and matches are that tool's suggestions, not decisions from an employer.

A safer editing instruction is: preserve the facts; flag unsupported claims; suggest clearer wording using only the evidence supplied; ask about missing details rather than inventing them. Keep the original so you can compare. The cover-letter checker can prompt a wording review, but cannot verify a vacancy, your experience or the truth of a claim.

7. Prepare the application, then check the destination

Fictional supporting statement

Answer for the practice form, within its 120-word limit

I am applying for the Operations Administrator practice role because its focus on accurate records and clear handovers matches my office-assistant experience. At Cedar Parcel Services, I maintained an Excel exception log, checked order references and flagged missing status information. I drafted customer updates from confirmed information and passed unresolved cases to the operations lead with the last action and next check. I also prepared a weekly outstanding-items summary. I use Excel sorting and filters, but I have not used an ERP system and would need training. I would welcome clarification of the shift pattern and expected start date.

This answer uses the same evidence as the CV and names the software gap. It does not invent enthusiasm for a company's mission, a conversation with a recruiter or eligibility to start. National Careers Service application advice supports relevant examples, checking answers and keeping a copy. The 120-word rule is invented for this exercise, not a UAE standard.

National Careers Service cover-letter advice supports a role reference, relevant evidence, factual wording and checking recipient details. The real advert decides whether to use a portal, letter, email or another route. Do not send a duplicate email just because this workshop has an example.

Final send check

  • The verified employer, role reference and actual recipient or portal match the advert. Use the employer-verification guide if the route or a payment request is unclear.
  • Every date, title, qualification and number is consistent across the form, CV and statement.
  • No example name, employer, invented result or placeholder remains in your own application.
  • The exported document opens, extracts into readable text and follows the actual file instructions.
  • Each unresolved essential requirement has been addressed honestly, not hidden by keyword matching.
  • Any promised attachment is actually attached and is the version you reviewed.

Completed practice record

Role: JP-PRACTICE-01, fictional Operations Administrator. Materials prepared: fictional CV and practice statement. Destination: none, exercise only. Status: drafted, not submitted. Unresolved: shifts, start date, work permission and ERP training. Outcome: none. No reply, interview or improvement in hiring chances has been measured.

For a real application, save the sent files, route and date privately. Record "submitted" only after the service confirms submission or your send succeeds. Keep follow-up consistent with the employer's instructions. The job-search playbook connects this application step to the wider search.
