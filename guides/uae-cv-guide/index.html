UAE CV Guide

By Ahmed Abayzeed · Gulf Job Hub · Last updated: August 21, 2026

A CV that works well in the UAE isn't wildly different from a good CV anywhere — but a few local conventions trip people up, especially candidates applying from markets where those details are usually omitted.

Length and format

Keep it to one or two pages. Use a clean, single-column layout that's easy to scan — many recruiters review dozens of CVs a day and skim before they read. Reverse-chronological order (most recent role first) is the norm.

What UAE employers expect to see

  • Nationality and visa/residency status — this is far more prominent here than in most Western markets, since it directly affects whether an employer needs to sponsor you. State it plainly near the top.
  • Notice period — how soon you can start if currently employed. Recruiters will ask if it's missing, so save the back-and-forth.
  • Languages spoken — especially Arabic and English proficiency, relevant for many client-facing and government-adjacent roles.
  • A contact number reachable on WhatsApp — a large share of UAE recruiter communication happens there.

Structure that works

  1. Header: name, title, phone/WhatsApp, email, LinkedIn, visa status
  2. Short professional summary (3–4 lines, tailored to the role)
  3. Work experience, with achievement-focused bullets rather than duty lists
  4. Education and relevant certifications
  5. Skills and languages

The photo question

Including a professional headshot photo is optional and not a legal requirement, but it remains common practice in the region, particularly for hospitality, retail, and customer-facing roles. Corporate, tech, and finance CVs increasingly go without one. When in doubt, check what's typical for your specific industry rather than defaulting either way.

What to leave out

Skip salary history — the UAE market negotiates this separately, and stating it early can anchor you lower than necessary. There's no need for references on the CV itself ("available on request" is standard). Avoid dense paragraphs; recruiters respond better to scannable bullets.

Tailor the keywords

Many UAE recruiters and larger employers use applicant tracking software that scans for keywords from the job description. Mirror the exact phrasing used in the listing (e.g. "stakeholder management" vs "client relations") where it genuinely applies to your experience.

This is general guidance based on common regional practice, not a template guaranteed to suit every industry or employer. When possible, ask someone currently working in your target field or company for a sense of local norms.
