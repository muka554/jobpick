# JobPick AdSense and High-Value Content Strategy

## Purpose

JobPick should be positioned as an independent Middle East job-search information service, not as a thin directory of outbound links. The objective is to give job seekers original, practical information that helps them choose a search route, evaluate a vacancy, prepare an application, and avoid recruitment fraud. More pages are not automatically better: each indexable page should answer a distinct user need and should be worth bookmarking even if the reader never clicks an external job platform.

Google’s current people-first guidance emphasizes original information, substantial coverage, evidence of expertise, clear authorship, and content created for people rather than search engines. It also warns against producing many pages across topics primarily to capture search traffic or rewriting other sources without adding meaningful value. These principles align closely with AdSense’s low-value-content warning.

## Editorial standards to enforce on every page

1. **Distinct purpose.** Before publication, record the user question, intended audience, primary source set, and why the page is different from an existing page. Merge or redirect pages that do not have a meaningful distinction.
2. **Named responsibility.** Every guide should identify the author or editorial team, publication date, last substantial review date, and what changed during the review. The author page should explain relevant experience without overstating qualifications.
3. **Source transparency.** Cite and link to official government, employer, platform, or regulatory sources. Record the source title, URL, date checked, and the specific claim supported. Do not copy large passages from sources.
4. **Original analysis.** Add a decision framework, comparison, example, checklist, or first-hand observation. A list of links, a paraphrase of another article, or a generic AI-generated summary is not enough.
5. **Truthful freshness.** Change `dateModified` only when the content materially changes. Add a visible “what changed” note for meaningful updates. Do not rotate dates to simulate freshness.
6. **Vacancy separation.** Clearly distinguish JobPick’s editorial guidance from third-party job listings. Do not imply that JobPick verifies, endorses, or represents an employer unless a documented partnership exists.
7. **Safety and YMYL care.** Employment, immigration, payment, and personal-data guidance should be cautious, current, and linked to official sources. Avoid legal conclusions and avoid guarantees about hiring, visas, salaries, or approval outcomes.
8. **Useful internal links.** Link from each guide to the next practical step: a related city or role guide, the methodology, the safety checklist, and the relevant official route. Avoid repetitive exact-match link blocks.
9. **Ad placement discipline.** Keep ads away from navigation controls, download buttons, outbound application buttons, and interactive search results. A reader should be able to identify the publisher content and complete the page’s task without clicking an ad.
10. **Quality gate.** Do not publish a page until it has a useful title and H1, a complete body, a real canonical URL, author/review metadata, working internal links, valid structured data where appropriate, mobile readability, and a sitemap entry only if it is ready for indexing.

## Pages to prioritize next

The first three additions should be the UAE Job Search Playbook, the UAE employer-verification guide, and a genuinely differentiated Dubai-versus-Abu-Dhabi job-search comparison. They strengthen the site’s core purpose, add first-party decision support around the existing search directory, and create natural internal links to the current UAE, city, role, safety, methodology, author, and contact pages.

The current role landing pages should be reviewed before expanding the cluster. If a page mainly repeats a short introduction and points to external searches, enrich it with role-specific preparation and evidence guidance or consolidate it into a broader role-family guide. Avoid generating dozens of city/role combinations solely because they have keyword demand.

## Measurement and review cadence

Use Search Console to monitor impressions, clicks, indexing state, and queries by page group rather than judging success by indexed URL count alone. Review the sitemap and the indexing report weekly after a material release, then monthly. Maintain a simple content register containing page owner, purpose, sources, last substantial review, next review date, indexed state, and traffic trend.

Approval is not guaranteed by word count, a particular number of pages, or structured data. The strongest long-term signal is a coherent site where a reader can arrive on a page, learn something original and complete, and understand who produced the information and how it was checked.
