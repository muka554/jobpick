A personal story

Why I built this site

By the site creator · Published 21 November 2026 · First-person account

For me, this is the story of a dreaming kid trying to make something useful out of a frustrating experience. I want finding a job to be easier, for me and for other people who are trying to find their next opportunity.

The reason is personal. Around April or May, the project I was working on closed. I was still with the same company, but before moving to another job inside it, I was afraid I might lose my job altogether. I started looking for work outside the company because I did not know what would happen next.

That search was really hard. Eventually, I received another offer inside the same company. My situation moved forward, but the difficulty of searching stayed with me. I had also found many other people facing a similar situation. The uncertainty was not something I was experiencing alone.

When I talk about that period, the fear matters as much as the search itself. I was working, a project had closed, and I was worried about what would come next. I did not yet have the certainty of another role. That was the point when I started searching outside.

I do not want to make my experience sound bigger or more dramatic than it was. I received another offer within the same company. I was not describing a confirmed dismissal. But the possibility of losing my job was enough to make the search feel urgent and difficult.

That distinction is important to me. Someone does not need to have already lost their job for the uncertainty to feel real. In my case, the worry came before I knew how the next step would work out. When I found other people in similar situations, I could see why it was hard for them to concentrate too. I cannot speak for every person looking for work. My experience is one experience, and the people I came across do not represent the whole job market. What I can say is that the frustration gave me a reason to try to help.

That is the idea behind this site. I am trying to make the process easier to approach. I do not want to pretend that putting information on a website removes the difficulty of finding a job. It does not. But I want this to be a useful place for someone who is trying to work out their next step.

I am part of that audience myself. When I say I want to make it easier for everyone else, I also mean that I want to make it easier for me. This started with a problem I recognised, not with a claim that I had already solved it.

Receiving another offer inside my company was the outcome of my own situation. It is not evidence that following my path will produce the same result for someone else. I do not want to turn it into a formula for getting hired.

The site cannot promise a vacancy, an interview or an offer. An employer's decisions are not mine to make. A personal story should not be used to suggest otherwise. I would rather be clear about that than give someone certainty I cannot provide.

The ambition is simpler: to try to make a difficult search a little easier to understand. That is a purpose I can keep working towards without pretending the work is finished.

The phrase I come back to is "a dreaming kid". It describes the hope behind this better than a big success story would. I had a difficult experience, found other people facing similar uncertainty, and wanted to do something useful with it.

That is why I built this site. I am still trying to make finding a job easier, for myself and for other people. I do not have a perfect answer. I have a reason to keep trying.
